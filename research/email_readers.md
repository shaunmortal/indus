Subject: A reproducible structural account of the Indus seal texts — request for a critical read (independent researcher, India)

Dear Dr Frenez,

I am an independent researcher in India. Over the past weeks I have built, with AI assistance, a fully open and reproducible analysis of the two machine-readable Indus corpora (Mahadevan 1977 and ICIT/Wells) together with the object metadata (seal dimensions, shape, emblem, find-spot, period). Everything is scripted and archived: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23396758 (code, data tables, notes) and https://github.com/shaunmortal/indus.

The main results, each tested with permutation nulls and multiple-comparison correction, are in a short paper draft (research/paper_v0.3.md):
- the short and tall stroke signs do not behave as a number system (values peak at 7 and 12; no place-value order; the two classes take disjoint complements; replicated on both corpora);
- graphic resemblance between signs carries no information about their use (r = 0.05 over 5,356 pairs), and an allograph test defended by held-out prediction gives corrected sign lists;
- seal text content is independent of emblem, excavation area and period, but round seals carry a distinct small lexicon, and the handled jar M343 sits on seals about 25% smaller within site, length and emblem strata;
- 96% of square-seal texts are unique identifiers, with no inflectional alternation at the end (11 sd fewer than chance).

I write to you because of your work on seals as markers of identity and on the Indus-related seals abroad, and because several of these results bear directly on your January 2026 paper on the oligopolistic, branding character of the seal system: the text channel and the motif channel appear to be statistically independent, the text is individual (unique) while the motif is standardised, and repeated title-like texts sit on smaller seals. I also re-read the 'hybrid-back' steatite stamps in Legrain's Ur volume (UE X nos. 636-650) in connection with your 2014 'Hybrid Identities' paper that I could not find in print.

I would be grateful for a critical read of the draft, however brief, and for any correction of fact or method. I make no claim of decipherment; the aim is to fix what kind of text a seal carries before any reading is attempted. I would of course acknowledge your comments.

With respect and thanks,

Subject: ICIT-based results on the Indus sign list and stroke signs — request for comments and ICIT access

Dear Dr Fuls,

I am an independent researcher in India working on the Indus script with open, reproducible methods. Using the ICIT transcription that is openly available through the indus-website database, together with Mahadevan's corpus, I have tested several long-standing claims with permutation nulls and held-out prediction. The archive is at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23396758 and the draft at https://github.com/shaunmortal/indus/blob/main/research/paper_v0.3.md.

Two results bear directly on your work. First, an objective allograph test (drawing similarity against distributional similarity, validated by out-of-sample perplexity) applied to the Wells sign list merges the fish variants and, notably, W817 with W861, while keeping apart several pairs that look alike (e.g. W690/W692, W621/W632); the merges lower held-out perplexity from 37.7 to 32.0 against 36.9 for random merges. Second, the stroke signs: the 'long stroke = 10' reading fails three tests on both corpora, and one and two tall strokes prove interchangeable in context by prediction.

I would value your comments and corrections, and I would also like to ask whether access to the full ICIT (sign images and the complete text set) could be granted for this work, with proper citation of ICIT in anything that results.

Subject: Stroke signs and sign classes in the Indus corpus — a reproducible follow-up to your 2019 paper

Dear Dr Yadav,

I am an independent researcher in India. Your 2019 paper on the stroke signs and your work with Dr Vahia on sign structure are the starting point of a set of tests I have run on the open Mahadevan and ICIT corpora, all scripted and archived (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23396758). On the stroke signs I find that the value distribution is non-monotone (peaks at 7 and 12), that compound groups have no fixed order, that tall and short strokes take disjoint sets of following signs, and that one-row and two-tier forms of the same count behave as different signs; on two-sided tablets the tall strokes sit on side 2 with the cup sign. I also find that only three function classes (opener, core, closer) are stable across resamples.

I would be grateful for your critical reading of the short draft (research/paper_v0.3.md in the repository), especially where it disagrees with your own counts, and for any correction.

One figure in my earlier message was wrong and I want to correct it before you spend time on it. I wrote that final-sign alternation on shared stems was '11 sd fewer than chance'.
