## 1. Dr Dennys Frenez — Independent scholar c/o Dept of History and Cultures, University of Bologna (dennys.frenez@unibo.it, from his Jan 2026 SSRN paper; the IITGn address bounced on 2026-10-15)
Subject: A reproducible structural account of the Indus seal texts — request for a critical read (independent researcher, India)

Dear Dr Frenez,

I am an independent researcher in India. Over the past weeks I have built, with AI assistance, a fully open and reproducible analysis of the two machine-readable Indus corpora (Mahadevan 1977 and ICIT/Wells) together with the object metadata (seal dimensions, shape, emblem, find-spot, period). Everything is scripted and archived: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23396758 (code, data tables, notes) and https://github.com/shaunmortal/indus.

The main results, each tested with permutation nulls and multiple-comparison correction, are in a short paper draft (research/paper_v0.3.md):
- the short and tall stroke signs do not behave as a number system (values peak at 7 and 12; no place-value order; the two classes take disjoint complements; replicated on both corpora);
- graphic resemblance between signs carries no information about their use (r = 0.05 over 5,356 pairs), and an allograph test defended by held-out prediction gives corrected sign lists;
- seal text content is independent of emblem, excavation area and period, but round seals carry a distinct small lexicon, and the handled jar M343 sits on seals about 25% smaller within site, length and emblem strata;
- 96% of square-seal texts are unique identifiers, with no inflectional alternation at the end (11 sd fewer than chance).

I write to you because of your work on seals as markers of identity and on the Indus-related seals abroad, and because several of these results bear directly on your January 2026 paper on the oligopolistic, branding character of the seal system: the text channel and the motif channel appear to be statistically independent, the text is individual (unique) while the motif is standardised, and repeated title-like texts sit on smaller seals. I also re-read the 'hybrid-back' steatite stamps in Legrain's Ur volume (UE X nos. 636-650) in connection with your 2014 'Hybrid Identities' paper that I could not find in print.

I would be grateful for a critical read of the draft, however brief, and for any correction of fact or method. I make no claim of decipherment; the aim is to fix what kind of text a seal carries before any reading is attempted. I would of course acknowledge your comments.

With respect and thanks,

First, an objective allograph test (drawing similarity against distributional similarity, validated by out-of-sample perplexity) applied to the Wells sign list merges the fish variants and, notably, W817 with W861, while keeping apart several pairs that look alike (e.g. W690/W692, W621/W632); the merges lower held-out perplexity from 37.7 to 32.0 against 36.9 for random merges. One figure in my earlier message was wrong and I want to correct it before you spend time on it. I wrote that final-sign alternation on shared stems was '11 sd fewer than chance'. ## DRAFT email 4: Prof. Asko Parpola (asko.parpola@helsinki.fi — address from the Columbia AMESA directory and Indology list; verify on TUHAT before sending) — NOT SENT, Ayush to approve
Subject: The dots-in-circle side of the Harappa prism tablets: dot count = stroke count (8 of 9)

Dear Professor Parpola,

I am an independent researcher in Bhopal, India, working on the structure of the Indus seal texts with open, reproducible methods (repository and archived draft: github.com/shaunmortal/indus; doi:10.5281/zenodo.23399794). I write with one small observation that follows directly from your 2020 paper on the ivory-rod inscriptions, where you describe the triangular prisms H-352 to H-354 with the rod text, the 'VIII' side and the 'dots-in-circle' side.

Reading the CISI vol. 1 plates (pp. 230-232), two of us independently counted the dots on the third side and the strokes beside the cup on the second side of every tablet where both are legible. On the nine tablets that carry the cup formula (H-352, 353, 354, 356, 357, 359, 362 from the plates, and H-1942 as you describe it) the number of dots equals the number of strokes on eight; the exception is H-361 (two strokes, three dots). A coin would do that with p about 0.02. The dots take only the values 2 and 3 on these rows, the same closed set the strokes take on the tablets as a whole (values 2-4; 5 never occurs beside the cup on any distinct tablet text in the published corpora). The readings are listed object by object in data/third_side_circles_v1.json in the repository, with the two readers marked.

If the dots repeat the number, the third side is a second notation of the quantity rather than (or as well as) a solar motif, and 'two/three pots of offerings' acquires a cross-check inside the object itself. Nine tablets are too few to be sure. May I ask two things: (1) whether CISI vol. 3.1-3.2 photograph further three-sided tablets with a dots-in-circle side (H-2240, H-2241 and the HARP batch of 22), and whether a scan of those plates could be made available to me, since vol. 3 is not accessible in India; (2) whether, in your own reading, any tablet contradicts the equality beyond H-361.

Thank you for your work on the Corpus, which made this possible from a laptop.

With respect,
Ayush Kumar
