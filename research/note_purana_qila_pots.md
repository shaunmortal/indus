# Wells's volumetric anchor: the three Purana Qila jars, checked (2026-10-16)

Claim (Wells, Daily Telegraph / HNN 17 Nov 2009; Wells 2015 ch. 4, Table 4.1): three inscribed jars from Harappa kept in the Purana Qila reserve collection, New Delhi, carry 'V' + 3, 6 and 7 strokes, and their measured capacities stand in the ratio 3:6:7; hence the strokes count a unit of volume and the same formula on moulded tablets records quantities.

What we checked:
- The jars are CISI vol. 1 H-372, H-371, H-370 (plates pp. 233-234). Seen on the plates: H-370 seven long parallel strokes on the shoulder, no V; H-371 six strokes + a V of two diagonals; H-372 (smaller jar) three strokes + V. ICIT transcriptions [37], [36,700], [33,700] agree. The V is the same shape as the tablet 'cup' sign (W700 / M328).
- Capacities: only Wells has measured them; CISI gives no dimensions for pots. Three objects, one ratio.
- Corpus-wide tests (STATE F15, F33, F38, F39): on tablets the cup formula takes 2, 3, 4 (6 twice), rarely 1, never 5 on distinct texts (suggestive, registered as P17); the value is not fixed by the side-1 text and not a per-object free count. On pottery the tall strokes reach 4-7 more often than on seals (suggestive, P14); the high-value pots that can be checked on plates (M-614 five strokes + jar sign; Ai-7 four strokes + sign; H-371) are genuine sign texts.

Reading: the three-jar ratio is compatible with a volume unit but rests on three objects; the tablet evidence does not behave like free counts. A decisive test needs more measured vessels with cup + N (any new lot: measure capacity, score P14/P16/P17). This is the cheapest 'quantity anchor' in the whole problem: a museum with inscribed jars could settle it with a measuring jug.

## Addendum 2026-10-16: the third side (F40) and Parpola 2020
Parpola 2020 (ivory/bone rods paper, hdl 10138/600354; copy in data/raw/books/) already knew the three-sided prisms H-352-354 and called the third side a 'dots-in-circle' sun motif, reading the stroke side as 'two/three/four pots (of offerings)'. He did not compare counts. Our plate readings (Claude + Ayush independently) plus his description of H-1942 give: cup-formula tablets 9, dot count = stroke count on 8 (coin p = 0.020); all legible pairs 10, match 8. The one miss (H-361: two strokes, three dots) is clear on the plate. If the circles are a second notation of the number, then (a) the tablet value is a number, written twice; (b) the 'pots of offerings' reading gets a count it can be tested against; (c) P17/P16 (values 2-4) and the 2-3-only dot rows describe the same closed set. Growth: CISI 3.2 (Vats's remaining three-sided tablets, H-2240/2241, H-1942) and HARP's 22-tablet batch.
